07 / SHARE AND TELL A STORY

Publishing the report changes who can use it.

After you publish, other people may read, change, reuse or share the report. Check the file, the refresh and the permissions before you hand it over.

DESKTOP → SERVICE PUBLISH CHECK ACCESS

A report can be correct in Desktop and still go wrong after publishing. Refresh may fail, a link may reach the wrong people or a bookmark may hide useful context. Check the report and who can use it.

Permission is not one thing

V

View

Read the report.

E

Edit

Change the report.

B

Build

Make new reports from the tables behind it.

R

Reshare

Give the report to someone else.

Publish it, then check it

1

Check the local report

Check refresh, page titles, filters, numbers, limits and bookmarks before publishing.

You should see: a report ready for someone else to review.
Why: publishing gives the report a new audience, so check it before it leaves Desktop.
2

Choose the workspace

Write down the workspace and owner. The LMS may use My workspace as an example.

You should see: a named destination and an identified owner.
Why: someone must know where the report lives and who maintains it.
3

Publish from Desktop

Save the report, choose File → Publish → Publish to Power BI, and select the approved workspace.

You should see: the report and its data in the selected workspace.
Why: the Service has its own access and refresh settings.
4

Check settings and refresh

Check the source credentials, refresh schedule, owner and people who should see the report.

You should see: a clear answer to “when will this data update?”
Why: a refresh that cannot sign in leaves the report out of date.
5

Help the reader follow the page

Use bookmarks, page navigation and short notes to move from the overview to the evidence and the next question.

You should see: a reader can move through the report without guessing.
Why: a bookmark saves a view; it does not prove that the view is typical or that one thing caused another.
6

Test with a reader

Ask someone who did not build the report to say the question, dates, number, evidence and limit.

You should see: the reader can explain what the report supports without author translation.
Why: the report works when another person can understand it.

Check it again after publishing

Numbers

Totals, peaks, medians and filters still work as defined.

Story

Notes and bookmarks still match the current data.

Access

The right people can open it, and others cannot.

Final check before you share it
  1. Who owns the report?
  2. Who can view, edit, build from or reshare it?
  3. When will the data refresh, and will sign-in work?
  4. Can a reader tell what the chart shows and what it cannot prove?

Check: the report should still make sense after publishing, refreshing and changing the audience.

Code Institute LMS exercise: publish the finished project

The LMS example starts with your checked local RetailDashboard.pbix. Publish it, then check the report, refresh and access in Power BI Service.

Follow the LMS publication route

  1. Quality-check the local report, then choose File → Publish → Publish to Power BI.
  2. Select the workspace you mean to use (the LMS may demonstrate My workspace), and record the owner.
  3. Review credentials, refresh, sensitivity and permissions: View, Edit, Build and Reshare are different grants.
  4. Open the published report as a reader and test bookmarks, navigation, filters and annotations against the current data.
Source file: RetailDashboard.pbix.

Know what the report uses

The report uses three LMS sources: Features data set, sales data-set and stores data-set. Uploading the file does not prove that refresh or access works.

Reader test: ask someone who did not build the report to name the question, period, measure, evidence and limitation.
Source pack: Features data set.csv · sales data-set.csv · stores data-set.csv.
